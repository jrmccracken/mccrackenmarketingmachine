What is a fractional CMO?

A fractional CMO is an experienced Chief Marketing Officer or Marketing Director who works with your business part-time, on an ongoing basis. Typically that means one or two days a week, sometimes less once things are running well.

It isn’t consultancy where you get a report and a handshake. A fractional CMO sits in your leadership meetings, owns the marketing plan and budget, manages your team and agencies, and is accountable for what marketing delivers. You get the same seniority you’d get from a full-time hire, for the amount of time the business actually needs.

Signs you need one

Marketing is busy but not moving the numbers

Plenty of activity, a steady stream of posts and campaigns, but nobody can say what it’s doing for pipeline or revenue.

You have a team or agencies but no leader

A capable marketing manager or a couple of agencies, with no one senior setting direction, challenging the work or holding them to account.

The founder is still running marketing

The MD or a sales director signs off every campaign because there’s nobody else at the right level. It’s eating their time and it shows.

You’re preparing for growth or a sale

New funding, a new market, a product launch or an exit on the horizon, and the marketing needs to stand up to scrutiny from a board or a buyer.

What I’ll do

The first 90 days

  1. Weeks 1 to 4: understand the business. Talk to your leadership team, sales, a handful of customers and anyone doing marketing now. Review the numbers: where leads come from, what converts, what it costs.
  2. Weeks 5 to 8: set the strategy. Agree who you’re targeting, what you want to be known for, which channels matter and which to stop. Put it on a few pages the whole leadership team can read.
  3. Weeks 9 to 12: build the plan and fix the basics. A 12-month plan with budget, owners and measures. Sort tracking and reporting so everyone works from the same numbers. Take on the quick wins.

After that, month to month

  • Run the marketing plan and report to the leadership team or board each month.
  • Manage your marketing team and agencies: briefs, priorities, quality and performance.
  • Own the budget and move money towards what’s working.
  • Work with sales on lead quality, handover and messaging.
  • Hire and develop marketing staff, including the full-time leader who may eventually replace me.

When it isn’t the right fit

Fractional leadership works best when there’s someone, or some agency, to do the day-to-day work. If you need a pair of hands to write posts and run campaigns, you’ll get better value from a good marketing executive or an agency.

It also isn’t right if marketing needs a leader in the building five days a week, for example a large team in the middle of a restructure. In that case I’ll tell you, and can help you write the brief and hire the right full-time person.

Frequently asked questions

What is a fractional CMO?

An experienced Chief Marketing Officer or Marketing Director who works with your business part-time, usually one or two days a week, on an ongoing basis. They own the marketing strategy and lead the team and agencies, without the cost of a full-time executive hire.

How much does a fractional CMO cost in the UK?

Experienced fractional CMOs typically charge £600 to £1,500 per day. One to two days a week usually works out at around £3,000 to £8,000 a month. I quote a fixed monthly fee after an initial call.

What’s the difference between a fractional CMO and an interim CMO?

An interim CMO usually works full-time for a fixed period, often to cover a gap between permanent hires. A fractional CMO works part-time on an ongoing basis, often for a year or more.

How long does an engagement last?

Most start with a 90-day period to diagnose, set the strategy and get some quick wins, then continue month to month. Many businesses keep a fractional CMO for 12 to 24 months, until they’re ready to hire a full-time marketing leader.

Will you do the marketing work as well as the strategy?

My main job is leadership: strategy, priorities, budget, hiring and managing the people and agencies who do the work. Some hands-on work is normal, but if you mainly need delivery, an agency or a marketing manager is usually better value.
