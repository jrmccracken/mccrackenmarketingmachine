Customer Insight

How to Conduct Customer Interviews That Improve Marketing

By James McCracken FCIM15 August 20267 min read

Customer interviews can reveal the language, barriers and buying context that dashboards miss. Here is a practical structure for marketers.

Recruit for the decision you care about

Speak to people who have recently bought, considered, rejected or stopped using the offer. Ten relevant conversations are more useful than a large convenience sample.

Ask about what happened

Questions such as “What was happening when you started looking?” and “What nearly stopped you?” produce better evidence than asking people what they hypothetically want.

Do not pitch or defend

The interviewer is there to understand the person, not correct their perception. If something they say feels wrong, that may be exactly the evidence marketing needs.

Listen for language

Record the words customers use for the problem, outcome and risk. That language can improve propositions, sales material, search content and campaign briefs.

Compare patterns, not anecdotes

After several interviews, group repeated triggers, barriers, questions and proof points. Use direct quotes as evidence, but make decisions from patterns rather than the loudest story.
